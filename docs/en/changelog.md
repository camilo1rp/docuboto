> Domain glossary (Colombian SGDEA — *Sistema de Gestión de Documentos Electrónicos de Archivo*, electronic records management system): **radicado** = an officially registered document with a unique, immutable tracking number; **expediente** = a logical case file grouping related radicados; **TRD** (*Tabla de Retención Documental*) = document retention schedule; **TVD** (*Tabla de Valoración Documental*) = valuation schedule for an accumulated/backlog fonds (documentation already produced without archival criteria, typically from suppressed units or liquidated entities); **dependencia** = organizational unit; **FUID** (*Formato Único de Inventario Documental*) = standard documentary inventory form; **PQRS** = citizen petitions, complaints, claims and suggestions; **índice electrónico** = electronic index that gives the electronic expediente legal validity.

## [Unreleased]

### Baseline v0.01 — what the system does

> **How to read this file.** OrpycaMCP has not cut a release yet: everything below
> is one unpublished baseline. The Spanish changelog keeps the entry-by-entry
> record of how it was built — every audit finding, remediation and increment,
> in the project's working language. This section states what v0.01 **is**, which
> is what an evaluator coming from outside needs; the individual entries that
> follow it cover selected changes and are not a complete log.

**Records management core (E01/E02/E03/E07)** — Registration of incoming, outgoing
and internal documents with an immutable tracking number, assigned atomically per
year + type + tenant. Expedientes (case files) with a full lifecycle
(`open → closed → transferred`), foliation, ZIP export and metadata templates
validated against a JSON Schema per documentary type. Attachments are SHA-256
checksummed on upload, deduplicated per tenant, streamed in chunks (never buffered
whole), and served through document-service so every download passes the clearance
gate.

**Retention and disposition (E04)** — TRD (retention schedule) and TVD (valuation
schedule for accumulated fonds) as a single instrument with two discriminators,
sharing one convalidation circuit: Comité approval, Consejo convalidation, RUSD
registration, publication and repeal — each with its administrative act recorded,
immutable once convalidated (enforced by a database trigger, not only by the
application). Final disposition per radicado and per expediente, with a mandatory
justification on the destructive path.

**Electronic index and signature (E15/E06/E17)** — Append-only versioned XML index
(RT-15 profile v4) with a per-document fingerprint and independent verification.
The index is signed at closure with an institutional XAdES-B/T/LT/LTA signature
(local CA, timestamp, long-term validation material), and transfer actas carry
per-user personal signatures whose role is derived from identity, never chosen by
the client. Personal PKI credentials are enrolled, renewed and revoked in-system,
with a versioned key-encryption keyring and purge of revoked material.
**Not accredited**: this is art. 7 electronic signature, not art. 28 — no ONAC
certificate, no HSM under sole signer control, no accredited TSA.

**Preservation (E10/E12)** — Preservation plan, PREMIS events, AIP packaging
(BagIt/PREMIS) and real PDF/A validation through veraPDF. WORM protection via
MinIO Object-Lock in `COMPLIANCE` mode over the signed index and the transfer
acta, with retention derived from the retention schedule. Primary and secondary
transfers with fixity re-verification on receipt. **Not accredited**: the
repository itself is not an accredited preservation repository.

**Security (E08)** — Multi-tenant isolation by PostgreSQL schema, with the
`search_path` switched per request from JWT claims and MinIO buckets scoped per
tenant. RBAC plus security clearance (no-read-up) applied in the `WHERE` of every
query and inside every write — never as a prior check, so authorization and
mutation are one act. Hash-chained immutable `audit_log`, partitioned monthly and
hardened per partition. Publication is an explicit, justified and audited act
(Ley 1712/2014 art. 12): absence of a reservation decision no longer means
"public".

**Workflow, notifications and search (E05/E16/E09/E19)** — Routing between
dependencias with assignment, transfer, step completion, transaction execution and
rollback, plus automatic routing rules. Email and in-app notifications with a
dead-letter queue and replay. Full-text search embedded in PostgreSQL (no separate
search service), reports, public lookup by verification code, and IMAP mail
ingestion.

**Interoperability (E11/E20)** — OAI-PMH harvesting (Dublin Core and EAD 2002 /
ISAD(G)), CMIS browsing and content retrieval, interoperable export and import of
radicados preserving the original tracking number with mandatory fixity, signed
outbound webhooks and an inbound postal-operator callback with per-tenant HMAC.
Every public channel ships **off** and trims at the source through a single
chokepoint. **Stub**: the concrete connector for a given postal operator.

**AI (E18/E21)** — An MCP server exposing the gateway's capabilities as tools, a
conversational assistant, and a knowledge-service doing retrieval-augmented
generation over pgvector with sovereign embeddings, ACL pre-filtering that fails
closed, and answers that always carry their citations.

**Frontend (E22)** — SvelteKit application with OAuth2 PKCE login, around 40 screens
covering the tray, expedientes, signatures, transfers, physical archive, search,
reports and the entity's own administration. The access token never reaches
client-side JavaScript: every gateway call goes through SSR.

**Operations** — Production Docker Compose manifest verified against real
containers, nginx template, backup and restore programs exercised end to end
(including audit-chain verification after restore), upgrade and rollback
procedure, CI running lint, unit, real-database and frontend tests across all
eleven services, and per-request tracing propagated from the edge.

### Added

- `feat(archive,api-gateway,frontend)`: **the elimination batch — first of the five phases of the only remaining requirement** (RF-TRD-07)
  Elimination happens **by batch** because the record of proceedings and the Committee's endorsement belong to the batch. This phase proposes and **freezes the inventory**; publishing, endorsing, signing the record and executing secure deletion are the four that follow, and the last one **is not built**. `ejecutado` always travels as `false`.
  **A resource of its own, not an appendix of `/trd`**: a batch is a procedure with a lifecycle, and each transition has constancies enforced by `CHECK` — publishing without a deadline gives no time to object, and endorsing without a record, date and administrative act is an unverified assertion.
  **The inventory is frozen, and not as a convenience copy**: it is what gets published for third parties to object to, and the expediente is going to cease to exist. A trigger enforces it: **never editable**, and removable **only while the batch is a draft**.
  **Five admission conditions**: a confirmed due date (without a decision there is no proposal — the only barrier against automatic destruction); a disposition that **ends in destruction** (`E`, `S`, `M`; Conservación Total **never** enters); a frozen instrument that governs it; an **expired WORM lock**; and not already being in a live batch.
  **There are THREE locks, and the longest one wins.** An expediente may carry `worm_retain_until` on its signed index, its transfer record and its AIP; while one stands, COMPLIANCE mode lets **nobody** delete, not even the administrator. Taking the first one found would yield a batch that blows up on execution, with the Committee already convened.
  **Rejections are reported, not dropped.** Whoever proposes 40 and gets 31 must see which nine are missing and why. An expediente outside the proposer's clearance is reported as "not found", **indistinguishable from a non-existent one**.
  **With its door on the same day**: only rows already confirmed whose disposition destroys carry a checkbox, and the `409` with its reasons is forwarded as-is instead of collapsing into "could not create batch".
  Migration `archive tenant/047` validated against real PostgreSQL. 823 archive-service tests, 1828 frontend, svelte-check 0/0, lint green. **The five conditions verified by mutation one by one.**
  *Why*: preserving everything forever is not complying with the TRD, it is failing to apply it — *Impact*: `/api/v1/eliminacion/` joins the gateway. **Still nothing is deleted.**

- `feat(archive)`: **the sweep that runs on its own, and the alert that reaches whoever is not looking** (RF-TRD-08 + R.4.8)
  Fourth step of the increment that **destroys nothing**, closing the scheduled trigger. The due-disposition queue had to be visited; now the system sweeps by itself and **alerts whoever can decide**.
  **No new infrastructure was needed**: `archive-service` already had two background loops with exactly the right shape — a testable `run_once`, their own kill switch and interval, never dying on an exception. This is a third with the same mould, and its interval **is** the alert cadence, so there is no per-tenant "last sent" state to keep in sync. **What silences the alert is deciding, not having read it.**
  **The plan's recommendation was false and was corrected before building on it.** It said "start in-app", assuming E16 had a notifications channel. **It does not**: the frontend store is ephemeral session toasts and `notification-service` is email only. A toast is not an alert — it vanishes in four seconds and is seen only by someone already looking, precisely who the alert is not for.
  **The alert is composed PER RECIPIENT**, and there lay the trap the plan flagged in advance: the count is narrowed by the recipient's clearance. Telling someone with clearance for 9 that they have "14 pending" tells them **5 classified expedientes exist**. Verified against real PostgreSQL: a classified overdue expediente counts for clearance 3 and **not** for 1 or 2. Whoever has nothing due **gets no email**.
  **Recipients are resolved by RBAC query**, with the same criterion as the deciding endpoint's gate, never a configured mailing list: a list drifts from the permissions silently.
  **The email is deliberately poor**: a number, a deadline and where to go. No tracking numbers, no subjects, no series — email leaves the institution and lands in mailboxes the SGDEA does not govern. It also states that **coming due is not deleting**.
  Failures isolated per tenant **and per recipient**. A form guardian verifies the job **executes no disposition**. 808 archive-service tests, lint green, migrations `045`/`046` applied and verified on the demo tenant. Three mutations verified.
  *Why*: a queue you must remember to visit does not satisfy "alert authorized users" — *Impact*: a third background loop that sends email; it requires `NOTIFICATION_INTERNAL_TOKEN` and refuses to alert without it. **Still nothing is deleted.**

- `feat(archive,frontend)`: **the authorized user's confirmation — "no destruction is automatic"** (RF-TRD-06)
  Third step of the increment that **destroys nothing**, and the one that turns the due-disposition queue into more than information: an authorized user now **decides**. It is rule 4 of the TRD requirements — "no destruction is automatic without an authorized user's confirmation" — and until today it existed nowhere.
  **Confirming still deletes nothing, and the response says so.** `ejecutada: false` travels explicitly with a note, because a `201` on an endpoint called "decide" invites the belief that something happened; the screen's modal repeats it before the person clicks.
  **The ADR-025 D5 gate finally gets a caller.** `assert_disposicion_final_autorizada` had been written, tested and **called by nobody** — its own docstring said so. It now blocks confirming a deletion whose frozen instrument does not govern it, which is the right moment: rejecting it at the end of the procedure, once the Committee has been convened, would be discovering the limit by failing. **`CT` is exempt**; `E`, `S` and `M` are not.
  **What is not accepted from the client is the point.** The due date and disposition code do **not** travel in the body — the backend resolves them from the frozen snapshot; otherwise confirming a deletion would be a matter of sending the right date. Out-of-reach expedientes answer **404, not 403**.
  **Deferring is a decision, not hiding something** (Model SGDEA R.4.9): it requires a reason of at least 10 characters and a **future** return date, validated in the proxy, the schema and a migration `CHECK`. The item leaves the active queue and **comes back on its own**.
  Decisions live in `disposicion_decisiones` (migration `archive tenant/046`), **append-only**, storing the **frozen** due date they were made about — a 2026 confirmation cannot authorize a deletion a reformulated TRD orders in 2030. The immutable audit entry is written **in the same transaction**.
  Migration validated against real PostgreSQL. 801 archive-service tests, 1824 frontend, svelte-check 0/0, lint green. Five mutations verified separately.
  *Why*: a calendar that comes due with nobody deciding is an archive administering itself, and the rule requires the opposite — *Impact*: a new endpoint and two actions in the fourth tab of `/admin/trd`. **Still nothing is deleted.**

- `feat(archive,frontend)`: **the system stored the retention calendar and never said it out loud** — the due-disposition queue (RF-TRD-08)
  Second step of the increment that **destroys nothing**. On closing an expediente the system freezes its calendar — `fin_archivo_gestion`, `fin_archivo_central`, the disposition code and the act that validated the series — and there it stayed: correct data nobody ever read. `GET /api/v1/trd/disposicion-vencida` surfaces it, and a fourth tab in `/admin/trd` puts it in front of whoever decides. **An endpoint with no door is the pattern this review has been chasing all along**, so it ships with its own on day one.
  **Coming due is not an order to destroy, and the UI cannot let it read that way.** Under Conservación Total, the central archive coming due means transfer to the historical archive and preserve **forever**; showing that row beside the deletion ones undifferentiated would turn the queue into a "list of things to delete" containing precisely the expedientes that are never deleted. Every row therefore carries its due action, **derived from the frozen snapshot** and written by admission.
  **`gobierna_eliminacion` does not reimplement the gate.** The ADR-025 D5 criterion was extracted into a single predicate now shared by the `assert` that blocks the act and the queue that merely reports. Copying the condition into the query would have been reimplementing the thing under verification, and the divergence would surface the day someone touched one side.
  **The delicate part is the count, not the page.** No-read-up sits in the **same** condition as the rows, `COUNT` included: narrowing only the page would let the total betray how many classified expedientes are due. The query also leaves an aggregate trace (RF-BUS-10).
  **A non-existent colour token caught before it shipped**: the notice used `var(--op-danger)`, which does not exist (it is `--op-error`). The browser would have dropped the whole declaration **silently**.
  787 archive-service tests, 1811 frontend, svelte-check 0/0, lint green. Four mutations verified.
  *Why*: a calendar that lives only in the database governs nothing, because nobody looks at it — *Impact*: the institution sees for the first time what came due, when, under which act and what is due. **No act is executed**: effective deletion (RF-TRD-07) remains unbuilt.

- `fix(archive)`: **an expediente closed without a TRD series left the lifecycle silently**
  First increment of the largest remaining product gap: **final disposition is never executed**. The system has the calendar — on closing it freezes `fin_archivo_gestion`, `fin_archivo_central`, the disposition code and the act that validated the series into `expedientes.disposition` — and it has the authorizing gate (`assert_disposicion_final_autorizada`, ADR-025 D5, **written and with no caller**). What it lacks is the executing arm. This increment is deliberately **the one that destroys nothing**.
  **And looking at it surfaced an older hole.** Closing an expediente **without a TRD series** left `disposition = NULL` and `trd_revision_requerida = false`: no retention calendar, no disposition date and **absent from every queue**. That is not an over-preserved expediente — it is one that leaves the archival lifecycle silently, because nothing names it and nobody will look at it again. Confirmed on the demo tenant: **1 expediente in that state**, invisible to `/trd/revision-pendiente`.
  The `cierre_sin_snapshot_de_retencion` reason **had existed since migration 037**, which used it to mark historical closures; the third tab of `/admin/trd` knew how to present it; and **no code path ever wrote it afterwards**. The backfill repaired the past and **nothing protected the future** — precisely the hole `audit_log`'s three defences had, covering modification and deletion with none covering insertion.
  **The fix is two halves, and the first alone would have been inert.** The service now marks the calendar-less closure; but the revision marks lived inside `if pin_trd:`, true only when the expediente **has** a series — exactly the opposite case. They are now written **on close**; on **transfer** they are still left alone, because the method's defaults would erase a pending mark nobody has resolved. Both halves verified by mutation **separately**.
  It **marks, it does not block**, the same criterion this method already applies to `migrada` series (ADR-025 D6). Migration `archive tenant/045` sweeps what slipped through between 037 and today, idempotent and validated against real PostgreSQL. 776 archive-service tests, lint green.
  *Why*: an expediente with no retention calendar is not preserved, it is unwatched — *Impact*: closures without a TRD series now surface in the third tab of `/admin/trd`, and the migration collects the earlier ones.

- `fix(archive,tenant,frontend)`: **the system was hiding the PINAR and the TRD while serving them**
  Closes the product decision that the negative sweep (B13) left open in `docs/es/plan-pruebas.md` §4.bis rather than settling on its own. The question: TRD and PINAR reads required no permission at all, while the sidebar hid the whole Administration section and `/admin` gated its cards. Gate them, or declare them open — both were legitimate — but **what was there was not defensible**.
  **The first thing that changed was the size of the problem.** The §4.bis count — "TRD 4, PINAR 11" — was written by hand. A census BY FORM over both services' routers found **33 ungated reads** reachable through the gateway: TRD's 4, **4 TVD ones the question never mentioned**, PINAR's 11, and 14 catalog reads.
  **With the full list the dilemma dissolved**, because those reads are not one thing. The **classification vocabulary** — TRD series and TVD groupings (list and detail), retention, document types, dependencias, catalogs, parameters — **stays open to any authenticated user of the tenant, now declared in code and covered by a test**: `/radicar` and `/expedientes` consume it in order to classify, and `USUA_PERM_TRD` belongs to whoever **administers** the instrument, not to whoever uses it. The validated instrument must be published anyway (Ac. AGN 004/2019, compiled by 001/2024; Ley 1712/2014 art. 9).
  The **circuit** is gated with `USUA_PERM_TRD`: `GET /trd/{code}/versiones` and its TVD twin return the full history — drafts, `acta_comite`, `fecha_aprobacion_comite`, `instancia_convalidante` and `created_by` of every version — the Committee's deliberation, not the published instrument. Its twin `GET /revision-pendiente` already required it.
  **PINAR's 11 reads now require `USUA_PERM_ADMIN`**, gated on the `APIRouter` itself — by admission, so tomorrow's endpoint inherits it — and **deleting the docstring sentence** that declared the opposite: a stale comment is the vector, not decoration. It closes rather than opens because a plan in `borrador` is the Committee's working material, and because publishing the approved PINAR (Decreto 1080/2015 art. 2.8.2.5.8) is an act directed outwards — a public screen — not an ungated internal GET that resembles one.
  On the frontend, `/admin/pinar`'s `load` put the `403` in the same bucket as network failures; it now travels as `sinPermiso`, the screen names the missing permission, and it **obeys the backend even when the session disagrees**.
  773 archive-service tests, 117 tenant-service, 1804 frontend; all five fixes verified by mutation.
  *Why*: a system that hides something and serves it at the same time has decided nothing — *Impact*: **PINAR's 11 reads and the two TRD/TVD version histories now answer 403 without the permission**; the classification vocabulary is unchanged and `/radicar` behaves as before.

- `fix(frontend)`: **an unreachable inbox announced its list might be out of date**
  Found **in the browser**, not in a test, while validating the freshly added scope gate: `jefe` (an administrator) opened another officer's **personal** mailbox inbox by direct URL. The backend refused with the neutral `404` — correct, and the screen explained it well — but it **also** showed, in amber, "we cannot check for news, so this list may be out of date; refresh it by hand before registering". Over an inbox that does not exist, with no list and nothing to register. The poller kept asking every 12s and its `404` read as a transient failure: **an authorisation error presented as something else**, the same defect that had `/envios` present a `403` as "no records".
  With the inbox unreachable there is no polling, the poller-down notice stays quiet, and "Consultar el buzón" no longer offers an impossible act. All three consequences hang off a single condition, with a test that the condition does not vanish; verified by mutation and checked live: no polling calls on the other person's account, and polling **present** on one's own.
  *Why*: a message blaming a network failure for what is a refusal of access sends you looking for the fault where it is not — *Impact*: whoever opens an inbox that is not theirs reads one explanation, and it is the true one.

- `fix(document,frontend)`: **the mailbox inbox gate said who may register, not which mailbox**
  A security finding, surfaced while documenting the inbox endpoints and discovering they were not documented at all.
  **`PERM_RADI` authorises working with email; which mailbox is decided by a different cut** — the `WHERE` in `email_cuenta_repository`, carefully written and tested, which hides an officer's **personal** account even from the administrator. Listing the inbox, reserving, releasing and discarding went straight to the message by id, **bypassing it**: anyone with `PERM_RADI` in the institution could read the subject and sender of another officer's private mail and set it aside. Only `sincronizar` and `radicar` crossed the cut, because they needed it to open the IMAP session — that is, the gate reached where it was technically required, not where it was required for safety. This is the second time in this repository that a correct cut fails to reach the door next to it.
  It is closed with a **single** guard in the service — the check repeated at every endpoint is the one that will one day be missing — answering `404 cuenta_no_encontrada`, not `403`: telling them apart would turn the endpoint into an oracle of which mailboxes the institution has and who works them. Plus a second layer in the mutation's `WHERE`: `cuenta_id` binds the message to the mailbox, because otherwise reaching **one** would be enough to operate on any other's mail knowing only its id. Both layers verified by mutation separately.
  **Discarding a message now writes to the immutable chain**, in the same transaction and **not** best-effort: if `audit_log` cannot take it, the message is not discarded. Deciding that a received communication is **not** registered is as much a decision as registering it — it is the one that makes the document disappear from the archive — and until now it left only an `UPDATE` on an ordinary table, rewritable afterwards without a trace. The entry carries neither subject nor sender: a discarded message was never registered, so it has no classification level with which to cut its readability, and in `audit_log` every `USUA_PERM_ADMIN` would see it. The reason does travel, because it is the text of the decision, not of the document.
  **And yet another capability built and never offered**: `liberar()` had existed in the repository with its test since Increment 2 with no way to call it. The effect was that opening a message, seeing it was not yours and closing the screen left it locked **for fifteen minutes for the whole front desk**, with no way out but waiting. There is now an endpoint, closing the form releases the reservation, and your own row offers "Soltarlo".
  document-service's integration lane gains the `public.audit_log` bootstrap ported from archive-service: the entry's atomicity cannot be checked with a double, because the rollback is produced by Postgres. And `docs/{es,en}/api.md` finally document all **seven** inbox endpoints, cross-checked against the running service's OpenAPI — not read.
  466 document-service tests (14 integration ones over the inbox), lint green.
  *Why*: a permission that says "may register" does not say "may use this mailbox", and an officer's personal mail does not belong to the front desk — *Impact*: anyone without an assigned mailbox no longer reaches its inbox; personal accounts are visible only to their owner, from the inbox too.

- `refactor(document,frontend)`: **the single-mailbox legacy route (`IMAP_*`) is retired**
  Closes E19 Increment 3. Gone: the `IMAP_HOST`, `IMAP_PORT`, `IMAP_USER`, `IMAP_PASSWORD`, `IMAP_USE_SSL`, `IMAP_MAILBOX` and `IMAP_TENANT_SLUG` variables, the `GET /api/v1/ingest/email/estado` and `POST /api/v1/ingest/email/poll` endpoints, the `imaplib` client and ingestor behind them, and the "legacy mailbox" block of `/admin/correo` with its history table.
  **Not cosmetic cleanup: that route could not do the job.** One mailbox per deployment does not express what Acuerdo AGN 060/2001 art. 13 requires; the password lived in clear text in a container's environment; and above all it **registered every unread message in one go** — advertising and newsletters included — because it had nowhere to separate what gets registered from what does not. It is replaced by what the three increments built: one row per mailbox with its encrypted credential, its filtering folder, its "Radicados" folder, its mode and its responsible officer.
  **A contradiction disappears at the root, too.** The screen used to say "email ingestion is not configured" with a configured, working mailbox right below it: two sources of truth talking at once. That was patched by conditioning the notice; now the second source does not exist, which is the strong fix. The guardian that watched the condition now watches that.
  The record of what was registered from email **is not lost with the history table**: every radicado leaves its entry in the immutable audit trail, and each account's inbox keeps which message produced which number — and which mailbox it came from, something the poll history never distinguished.
  409 document-service tests (the 15 of the retired route go with it), 9 for `/admin/correo`, `svelte-check` 0/0, lint green.
  *Why*: keeping two ingestion routes forces a decision about which one wins, and the legacy one registered the advertising — *Impact*: **anyone with `IMAP_*` in their environment must configure their mailbox from `/admin/correo`**; the variables are ignored and both endpoints return 404.

- `fix(document,frontend)`: **an incoming document could be annulled with the permission alone — and that act does not exist for incoming documents**
  A domain correction from the user, opening an **axis this work had not been auditing**. Every permission review of these days answers "is this person authorised?". There is a prior question: "is this act possible on this kind of document?".
  **A radicado de Entrada is not annulled.** If a document entered the institution, it entered: that is a fact, not a revisable decision. Annulment exists for what the institution **produces** — a Salida or Interno issued by mistake. The service only checked existence and prior annulment, so `PERM_SOL_ANULAR` was enough to annul an Entrada. Legacy Orfeo did distinguish (`sgd_trad_codigo`).
  The rule lives in `app/core/reglas_tipo.py`, written by **admission** (declare which types admit each act, never which are excluded — with an exclusion list the omission lands on the permissive side and says nothing). **Not even ROOT bypasses it**: ROOT's bypass is over authorisation, not over what is possible. `409`, not `403`: the caller is not missing a permission — granting one would change nothing.
  Both halves fixed: the inbox offered "Solicitar anulación" looking only at the permission, so it painted the button on an Entrada.
  *Impact*: `409 acto_no_aplica_al_tipo` on an Entrada, with or without permission, with or without ROOT. Verified by mutation in both layers.
  **Open**: this is the first rule of its kind to be declared. If there are more per-type particularities, `reglas_tipo.py` is now where they belong — and enumerating them at once beats discovering them one at a time.

- `fix(document,auth)`: **the permission catalogue promised nine controls and enforced one**

### Fixed

- `fix(auth,frontend)`: **the interface offered actions the server was going to reject, because it did not know what level each permission was granted at (third batch of the user-experience review)** — a permission in this system is not a yes or a no: it has a **level** — look up, edit, create or delete. The server knows it and enforces it; the interface never received it, because the lookup that tells it who the user is resolved the level and then **threw it away**, sending only the list of names. The result: someone holding a read-only permission was offered Close, Transfer, Exclude or Sign acta buttons, and got a "you do not have permission" when they pressed them. The two user-experience reviews, carried out separately and blind to each other, reached the same conclusion, and **both refused to fix it screen by screen**: the problem was not in the screens but in what the server tells them. It was fixed in both halves. The server now also sends the level of each permission, **without removing anything it already sent**, so nothing already running breaks and no coordinated update is needed. And the interface learns to ask by level, with a deliberately strict rule: if the level does not arrive — a session opened before this change, for instance — the answer is **no**, never "I don't know, so I'll allow it". Applying it meant reviewing **every** button that writes, one by one, against what its service actually requires. That corrected the finding's own premise: most operations only ask for the lowest level, so **only four screens** needed the high restriction. Requiring more would have been the same mistake in reverse, hiding functions from people who are entitled to use them — and doing so silently. It was noted, without being touched, that the inbox has exactly that shape: the interface asks for a permission the server does not require, because there whoever may act is decided by holding the assigned step, not by a permission. — *Why*: the interface must be a faithful mirror of what the server authorizes; offering what is going to be rejected is a functional failure, not a cosmetic one. — *Impact*: no database changes. 199 authentication-service tests and 1370 frontend tests green, including one that reproduces exactly the reported case.
- `fix(frontend)`: **accessibility: insufficient contrast, tables unreachable without a mouse, errors not announced on their field, and AI suggestions that were never announced (second batch of the user-experience review)** — four problems that left part of the users out. **One**: white text on the brand green did not reach the minimum required contrast, and not in a transient effect but in permanent states — the current step number in the two-factor, signature-credential and signing wizards; the active turn in the signature chain; the active filters in drafts and dispatches. In several places it was **the other way round**: the legible tone was used only on mouse-over. **Two**: wide tables cut off the Status and Actions columns on the right, and those that did scroll could not be scrolled with the keyboard, so anyone not using a mouse could not reach them. It was fixed in the two shared table components, so the fix reaches most screens. **Three**: on the profile screen, all seven validation error messages appeared only as a floating notice and never next to the failing field — precisely on the screen that governs the electronic signature credential; they are now announced on the field, and the one-time codes open the numeric keypad and autofill. **Four**: AI suggestions appeared without notifying screen-reader users; they are now announced, without touching the content, which already met the regulatory requirement to cite sources and warn that it was AI-generated. Two honest caveats: the table containers left unmodified wrap a component whose scrolling is already reachable, and giving them another keyboard stop would have created an empty destination; and the original finding mentioned four tables with one cell too many, which a sweep of all 28 tables in the project **could not reproduce** — recorded as such rather than forcing the figure. — *Why*: an actions column invisible on mobile, or an error a screen reader cannot locate, are not finishing details: they prevent the task from being completed. — *Impact*: 1348 tests green. The two-factor recovery code is deliberately left out: it is a server-side gap and must not be papered over in the interface.
- `fix(frontend)`: **batch loading never reported its result, three administration cards led to "no permission", and two screens were not linked (UX review, first batch of fixes)** — the ~20 screens built to surface everything the backend already exposed were implemented without going through the UX/accessibility review, the last step of the project's process. The review was done afterwards and found three **functional** problems, not cosmetic ones; this is its first batch of fixes. **One**: document and expediente batch loading never reported its result, due to two compounding defects — the progress panel did not refresh, and the screen polled progress at an address that had ceased to exist when the routing was fixed. An administrator could load a thousand radicados and have no way of knowing whether they were created or whether everything failed. **Two**: three cards in the administration panel advertised a permission different from the one the server actually requires, so they led to a "you do not have permission" screen; the Interoperability case was doubly wrong, because it also hid the entire screen from a registration operator who could use part of it. All **sixteen** cards were checked, not just the three found, because this same pattern had already resurfaced four times. **Three**: two finished screens — digital preservation and signing keys — were missing from the administration index, which presents itself as the complete map, and were reachable only by typing the address by hand. The root cause of the first failure was fixed too: several code comments asserted things that had stopped being true (that the gateway did not route batch loading, that the metadata services did not require a permission). This is the third time in this release that a stale comment has caused a real defect, so they were deleted rather than left sitting next to the fix, and tests were added that pin the exact address each screen asks the server for, so a route change cannot silently break it again. — *Why*: a screen signed off as finished and documented as complete had its main function inoperative; building the UI reveals the capabilities, but only the review reveals the UI. — *Impact*: batch loading reports its result; the administration panel stops offering what it will deny and stops hiding what it authorizes; two screens stop being invisible. 1339 frontend tests green.
- `fix(repo)`: **four files of the interoperability screen never made it into the repository** — an exclusion rule written for one specific folder at the project root was written unanchored, so it applied to **any** folder with that name anywhere in the tree. It matched the folder holding the document batch-loading files, which therefore stayed out of the history despite being documented as delivered: in the official copy of the project, the screen called addresses that did not exist there. Fixed by anchoring the rule to the root. — *Why*: it surfaced when re-running the work on a clean copy; the earlier review could not see it, because it reads the working files and there they were present. — *Impact*: none on application behavior. The lesson is that this kind of exclusion fails **silently**: the file simply never shows up as pending to save.
- `fix(document)`: **audit trail for batch radicado creation + reactivation of the batch test suite** — batch radicado creation (`POST /api/v1/batch/documents`) left no trace whatsoever in the immutable audit log: hundreds of official documents, each with a unique tracking number, could be created with no record of who did it or when. It surfaced because the twin batch operation over expedientes, in another service, had done it from day one — two mirror functions, only one leaving a trace. Fixed by writing one audit entry **per radicado created** (not one per batch, so individual traceability is not lost), always attributed to the person who submitted the batch, and in such a way that a partially successful batch (say 480 of 500) records exactly what went in and what did not — never the original intent. In addition, this feature's automated test suite had been fully disabled since it was written (it referenced a test fixture that never existed). It was rewritten from scratch, also covering the permission gate that had recently been added with no test verifying it. — *Why*: mass-creating official documents with no record of authorship is precisely what the audit log exists to prevent; a disabled test suite protects nothing, least of all a freshly added permission gate. — *Impact*: no schema change (the audit table already existed). document-service suite: 284 passing, 20 skipped (previously 273 passing, 25 skipped — the remaining 20 are integration tests against a real database, unrelated to this change).
- `fix(api-gateway,document,archive)`: **batch loading unreachable — missing gateway route and an ambiguous status contract between services** — closes the item left open by the interoperability screen: the gateway had no route registered for `/api/v1/batch/`, so both document and expediente batch loading always returned "route not found". Investigating it surfaced a deeper problem: the two services serving batch loading share the same base path, and the job-status lookup (`.../batch/{id}/status`) had **the identical shape** in both — there is no way for the gateway to guess, from the job id alone, which of the two to ask. The fix nests the status lookup under the job type (documents or expedientes), just as creation already did, so the routing decision is unambiguous. Along the way, document batch loading now requires the same permission as creating a document one at a time — it previously required none, unlike the expediente equivalent. — *Why*: without that route the already-built batch screen could not work; the contract shared by the two services had to be disambiguated at the source, not patched around. — *Impact*: batch status URLs change shape — and the screen consuming them kept the old shape, fixed later in the UX review entry; document batch loading gains permission enforcement. What this change left open — batch radicado creation without an audit entry — is **closed** by the first entry in this release.
- `fix(frontend)`: **accessibility sweep after the v1.0 redesign audit — 27 High/Critical findings (E22, WCAG 2.1 AA)** — AA contrast in `Button` and 8 screens, ≥44px touch targets in 8 components, visible focus in `UserPicker`, a misaligned column on mobile in `/busqueda`, and **explicit confirmation before signing** in `/firmas`, which had none.
- `fix(frontend)`: **redirect loop between Keycloak and `/login` (E22)** — an IdP error returned to `/login` restarted the flow indefinitely; the error is now mapped from a closed table and shown with a "Retry" link.
- `fix(frontend)`: **`//` comments inside `<style lang="scss">` broke the raw SSR build (E22)**.
- `fix(migrations)`: **idempotency guards that did not filter by schema broke the provisioning of the second tenant (multi-tenant)** — guards keyed on constraint/column names repeat across schemas, so from tenant #2 onwards the object was silently skipped. Two severities were found: missing constraints (backfillable) and, critically, DLQ migrations that **aborted** provisioning without marking themselves, requiring a re-run. - `fix(knowledge)`: **`POST /api/v1/knowledge/search` returned 500 against real PostgreSQL (E21)** — the connection pool lacked a `jsonb` codec; a pass-through encoder avoids double-encoding.
- `fix(scripts)`: **`init_tenant.py` provisioned incomplete tenants and aborted midway (E14/E21)** — two defects: migrations were skipped (knowledge + tenant-service, which silently left PINAR unusable) and migration identity was keyed by bare filename, so collisions between services (archive/document, notification/signature) raised a `UniqueViolation` halfway through provisioning.
